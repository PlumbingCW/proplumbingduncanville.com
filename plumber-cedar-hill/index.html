What Cedar Hill's hills and lake mean for your pipes

Cedar Hill has a different feel from the flat suburbs around it. According to Texas Parks and Wildlife, Cedar Hill State Park sits in the rugged, cedar covered hills of southwest Dallas County, and the park itself spans 1,826 acres on 7,500 acre Joe Pool Lake. Hilly lots mean sloped yards, retaining walls and drainage that moves water toward the foundation if it is not managed.

For plumbing, slope matters in two ways. A sewer line that has to climb or drop across a hillside has less margin for a belly or a shifted joint. And a lot that sheds water against a slab can move the soil, which stresses pipes under the house. When one of our plumbers arrives, the lot is part of the diagnosis.

If a pipe has burst or a leak is spreading, close the main water valve and call our 24/7 line. Our dispatch covers Cedar Hill every day, including nights and holidays, and a plumber is sent as soon as one is free.

Where Cedar Hill water comes from

Cedar Hill buys all of its water from Dallas, which treats it with chloramine, a mix of chlorine and ammonia. That is a useful fact in two ways. Water that reaches your tap has the same source as Duncanville, so water quality questions are answered by the same utility report. And chloramine matters if you shop for a filter, because the filter should be rated to reduce it.

The reports we reviewed give no hardness figure for Dallas water, so we do not state one. To see how your own water behaves, look at the scale on your fixtures and test your tap before you buy a softener.

Cedar Hill notes

Cedar Hill State Park's Penn Farm area dates to 1854, when John Anderson Penn settled in the Cedar Mountains area of southwest Dallas County. The state acquired the park in 1982 and opened it in 1991. Homes near parkland and the lake shore may have sprinkler and outdoor faucet lines that run long distances, and those are common leak sites.

Dallas Water Utilities reports do not list a hardness figure, so a tap test and the utility's annual report are the best way to learn how your water behaves.
