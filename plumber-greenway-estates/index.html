A closer look at Greenway Estates

Greenway Estates is a residential neighborhood in Duncanville, Texas, ZIP 75137. Listings in the area use Spring Lake Drive, Green Rock Drive, Green Terrace Lane and Rock Canyon Drive. No official boundary was found, so these streets are anchors and not edges.

Homes.com describes two-story and custom-built homes here, and its sample listings run from about 2,900 to 4,300 square feet. It also reports that about 96 percent of residents own their homes. That is third party data and does not describe any single house, so use it only to understand the general kind of home.

Two stories, more fixtures

A two-story house puts bathrooms above living space. Water from an upstairs leak travels through the floor and shows up as a stain or a bubble in the ceiling below, often away from the actual source. That is a point about house design and not a report of leaks in this neighborhood.

  • A ceiling stain or a damp wall below an upstairs bathroom. See leak detection.
  • A tub, shower or toilet upstairs that drains slowly. See drain cleaning.
  • A tank water heater in a large home that no longer keeps up with demand. See water heater replacement.
  • Interest in an on-demand unit for a house with several baths. See tankless water heater service.

Choosing a water heater for a larger house

Water heater size depends on how many people live in the house and how many fixtures run at once. A larger home often needs a bigger tank or a higher capacity unit. Tank units and tankless units are both options. A tankless electric unit needs a lot of power, so wiring falls under the National Electrical Code and a licensed electrician should confirm the panel can carry it.

If your heater is leaking from the tank itself, that usually means replacement and not repair. If it is leaking from a fitting or a valve, a repair may be enough. A plumber can tell the difference in person.

Yards and outdoor lines

Any yard has outdoor lines and spigots. A hose bib that drips, a sprinkler line that leaks or a wet patch in the yard with no rain can all point to a supply line problem. Reading your water meter with every tap off for a few minutes is a simple way to see whether water is moving when it should not be.
