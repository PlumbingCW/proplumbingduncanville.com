Jams, leaks and dead motors are repaired or replaced, with a clear price before the work begins.

A disposal that hums but does not spin, or one that leaks under the sink, is a kitchen emergency when dinner is half made. Turn the switch off, never reach into the unit even when it is off, and call our 24/7 line if water is leaking or the sink will not drain. A jam can wait for a morning visit, a leak cannot.

Garbage disposal repair covers jams, leaks, tripped resets, failed motors, loose mounts, clogged drains and installation of a replacement. We also tell you honestly when a disposal is worn out and replacement is the cheaper move.

Emergency: leaking or backing up

Water under the sink can come from the sink flange, the unit body, the dishwasher hose or the drain line. Each has a different fix. A leak at the body means the housing has failed and the unit needs replacement. A leak at the flange or the dishwasher connection is often a simple repair.

If the sink fills with water that will not drain, the problem may be a clogged line rather than the disposal. Do not run the disposal against a full sink. It will only chew debris in standing water.

Repair or replace a disposal

A disposal has a typical service life of several years. A motor that has failed or a housing that leaks is rarely worth repairing, because a new unit costs about as much as the labor of rebuilding. A jam or a worn seal is a quick fix.

When we replace one, we match the horsepower to your use. A single person who cooks lightly does not need the same unit as a family that cooks every night. We also check the drain, the power cord or hard wiring and the dishwasher connection while we are there.

  1. 01Power offWe switch off the unit and the breaker if it is hardwired.
  2. 02DiagnoseWe check for a jam, test the motor and look for leaks at each connection.
  3. 03Repair or replaceWe fix the fault or install a new unit with a new gasket and mount.
  4. 04TestWe run water, test the unit, check the dishwasher connection and clean up.

What decides the price of disposal repair

Disposal work is quick, and a clear price comes before work starts.

  • Repair or new unitClearing a jam is minor. A replacement adds the cost of the unit.
  • Horsepower and featuresLarger motors and sound insulation cost more.
  • WiringA unit with a plug is quick. Hardwired units may need an electrician for a new outlet.
  • Drain conditionA clog downstream is a separate drain job.

Ask for the price in writing and keep the receipt for the unit warranty.

What should never go in a disposal

  • Grease, oil and fat, which cool and coat the pipe
  • Coffee grounds and eggshells in large amounts
  • Potato peels, pasta and rice that swell
  • Bones, fruit pits and corn husks
  • Anything that is not food, including utensils and bottle caps

A disposal does not eliminate waste. It converts it to small pieces, and the drain still has to carry them. A kitchen line with years of grease is the most common reason a new disposal seems to fail soon after installation.

Many Duncanville kitchens were built decades ago, and the branch drains behind the sink are often metal or older plastic that collects grease. A disposal that works but drains slowly can be telling you about the line.
