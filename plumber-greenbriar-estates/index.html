Plumbing for larger family homes

A bigger house usually means more fixtures, longer runs of pipe and more water used at the same time. That is a general point about house size and not a claim about problems in this neighborhood. It does mean a few jobs are worth knowing about.

  • A kitchen or bathroom drain that slows down again soon after it was cleared. See drain cleaning.
  • Water that takes a long time to turn hot, or runs out, in a house with several baths. See water heater repair.
  • A wet spot, a rising water bill or the sound of running water with every tap off. See leak detection.
  • Repeated backups at the lowest drains in the house, which may point to the main sewer line. See sewer camera inspection.

Drains, trees and the main line

We have no data on how often roots or soil movement affect sewer lines in this neighborhood. In any yard with trees, roots, shifting soil and a worn pipe joint are the common causes of a main line that backs up.

If more than one drain is slow, or a toilet gurgles when the washer drains, the blockage may be in the main line and not in a fixture. A camera look shows where it is and what is causing it, and then the choice is between sewer line cleaning and sewer line repair.

Townhomes and shared walls

Where townhouses sit next to single-family homes, ask your association or owner which water lines are yours and which are shared. A leak in a shared wall is a good reason to close the unit shutoff and call right away.

Local notes

Duncanville adopted the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), and Permit and Inspection Services at (972) 780-5000 can say whether a water heater change or a sewer repair needs a permit. According to the Dallas Water Utilities 2022 water quality report, the supply is treated with chloramine.
