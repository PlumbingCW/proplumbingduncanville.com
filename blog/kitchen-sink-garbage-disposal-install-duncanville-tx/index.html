How to Install a Kitchen Sink and Garbage Disposal in Duncanville, TX

Parts, drain layout and the dishwasher connection for a new sink and disposal.

Call (972) 903-8224

A kitchen sink and a garbage disposal look like a weekend project, and in many homes they are. The parts are common, the steps are clear and the tools are basic. The details that matter are the drain layout, the dishwasher connection and the tightness of every joint under the sink.

This guide covers how a sink and disposal are installed, what goes wrong and when to hire a plumber. It is written for Duncanville homeowners who want to understand the steps, whether they do the work or watch it done.

Plan before you buy

  • Measure the cabinet and the sink opening, and note the number of bowls
  • Choose a disposal with enough horsepower for the way you cook
  • Check whether a power outlet exists under the sink, switched at the wall
  • Check the dishwasher drain hose and where it will connect
  • Look at the drain trap and the wall stub to see if they line up with the new sink

A new double bowl sink changes the drain layout. The drain from each bowl joins above the trap. Mixing a single bowl with a disposal is simpler. If you are moving the sink, the drain and vent may need changes.

Installing the sink

  1. 01Remove the old sinkShut off the water and disconnect the supply lines and the drain. Free the sink from the countertop and lift it out.
  2. 02Set the new sinkClean the opening, apply sealant or a gasket, set the sink and secure it with clips from below.
  3. 03Install the faucetMount the faucet and connect new supply lines with proper stop valves.
  4. 04Set the drainInstall the basket strainer or the disposal sink flange with plumber's putty or a gasket.

Use new supply lines, not the old ones. Hand tighten and add a quarter turn. Overtightening cracks fittings.

Installing the disposal

The disposal hangs from the sink flange with a mounting ring. After the flange is set, the mounting assembly is attached, and the disposal is lifted into place and locked. The discharge elbow connects to the trap, and a gasket is seated in each joint.

If a dishwasher is present, the knockout plug in the disposal inlet must be removed before the hose is connected. Forgetting this is the most common mistake. The dishwasher will not drain, and the plug is hard to remove after the unit is hung.

The dishwasher connection

A dishwasher drains through a hose that runs to the disposal or the sink tailpiece. The hose should rise in a high loop under the counter, or connect to an air gap, to stop sink water from flowing back into the dishwasher. A hose that sits low is a common cause of odors and backflow.

The hose clamp should be tight, and the hose should not kink. After installation, run the dishwasher and watch the connection for leaks.

Testing and common leaks

Fill the sink and release the water so every joint sees flow. Run your hand under each connection. Use dry paper towels to spot a weep. Leave a towel under the cabinet overnight and check it in the morning. A slow drip rots the cabinet bottom.

If a leak appears at the disposal body, the unit is faulty and should be replaced. If it leaks at the flange, reseat it. A leak at the trap is almost always a nut that needs to be tightened or a washer that is flipped.

Care after installation

A new disposal lasts longer when it is used properly. Run cold water before, during and for several seconds after each use, so the waste moves into the drain line. Feed it in small amounts rather than a full load at once. Keep grease and fibrous waste out, and skip bones and fruit pits. Clean it now and then by grinding a handful of ice or a few citrus peels.

If the unit jams, turn it off and use the hex socket at the bottom to free the flywheel, then press the reset button. If it hums and does not turn, or if it leaks at the body, call a plumber. Do not put a hand in the chamber for any reason.

Before you start, photograph the cabinet from the front with the doors open. The picture shows how the supply lines, the trap and the dishwasher hose are arranged, and it makes reassembly faster if something goes wrong.

Have a bucket, towels and a flashlight ready, and clear the cabinet floor so you can see any drip. Taking an extra ten minutes to prepare saves an hour of searching for a wrench in the dark.

Permits and electrical in Duncanville

A like for like sink and disposal swap may not need a permit, while new or moved plumbing often does. Permit rules vary, so ask Permit and Inspection Services at (972) 780-5000. The City of Duncanville adopted the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC).

If you hire help, ask for the Responsible Master Plumber license number and check it at tsbpe.texas.gov. A disposal needs a power source, and a licensed electrician should handle any new wiring.

When to hire a plumber

Hire a plumber if the sink is moving, if the drain is cast iron or galvanized, if you find a leak you cannot trace or if the cabinet is old and soft. A plumber also brings the tools to test and the experience to avoid callbacks.
