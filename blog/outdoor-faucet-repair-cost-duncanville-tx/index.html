Outdoor Faucet Repair Cost in Duncanville, TX

What decides the price of fixing a hose bib, from a washer to a split pipe.

Call (972) 903-8224

An outdoor faucet, also called a hose bib or spigot, is one of the simplest fixtures on a house, and one of the easiest to neglect. It sits outside, takes sun and weather, and often hides a leak inside the wall. The cost to fix one ranges from a small part to a wall repair, and what decides it is the cause.

This guide covers the common failures, what each repair involves, what moves the price and how to compare quotes. We do not list dollar figures, because they vary with the house and a made up range would mislead. We explain what you are paying for so the number your plumber gives you makes sense.

The four most common outdoor faucet problems

ProblemWhat you seeTypical repair
Dripping from the spoutWater drips when the faucet is closedReplace the washer or cartridge
Leak at the handleWater around the stem when openTighten the packing nut or replace the O ring
Leak at the wallWater behind the faucet, a wet stain belowReplace the faucet and check the pipe inside the wall
Split pipe from a freezeSpray or flow inside the wall when turned onOpen the wall, replace the pipe section, patch
No flowHandle turns but little water comes outClean or replace the valve, check the shutoff

The first two are small jobs. The third is a modest job. The fourth is the one that moves the price, because a split pipe inside a wall means opening the wall.

What drives the price

  • Whether the faucet is repaired or replaced. A washer is cheap, a frost proof bib costs more.
  • The wall type. Brick, stucco, siding and masonry each take different effort to open and patch.
  • The pipe behind the faucet. Copper, PEX and galvanized each use different parts.
  • Access from inside. If the pipe runs through a garage or crawl space, access is easy. If it is in a finished room, it is not.
  • The hour of the call. After hours work carries a quoted rate.

Replacing a faucet with a frost proof model is an upgrade that costs a little more up front and protects the pipe in a hard freeze. The valve seat of a frost proof bib sits inside the warm part of the wall, so the pipe outside holds no standing water.

Freeze damage and what it looks like

According to the City of Duncanville storm recovery page, Winter Storm Uri ran from February 13 to 17, 2021, and many Texas homeowners learned the hard way that a hose left on a spigot can freeze and crack the pipe behind it. The crack does not leak while the water is frozen. It shows up when things thaw and someone opens the faucet.

If you turn on an outdoor faucet and hear water inside the wall, or see a wet spot inside near the pipe, shut off the supply immediately and call. A small split in a pipe can pour for hours inside a wall and cause mold and rot.

Quote comparison tips

  1. Ask whether the quote includes opening and patching the wall.
  2. Ask whether the new faucet is frost proof.
  3. Ask what happens if the pipe behind the wall is also damaged.
  4. Ask for the price in writing before work starts.
  5. Ask whether the work has a warranty.

A quote that gives a single low number without describing the work may exclude the wall repair. Ask what is covered.

A quick spring and fall routine

Twice a year, walk around the house and test each outdoor faucet. Turn it on, look for a drip at the spout, a wet stem or a damp spot on the wall. Check the hose connection for a leak. In fall, disconnect and drain hoses, and in spring, check the faucet for freeze damage before the first big watering of the season.

This routine takes ten minutes and finds small problems early. A small drip repaired in spring is far cheaper than a split pipe repaired in summer after a wall has gone soft.

If you plan to sell the house, repair outdoor faucets before you list. A home inspector will test them, and a dripping or leaking bib becomes a negotiating point. A small repair now is easier than a request for a credit later. Keep the receipt, and note the date of the repair in your home file.

Disconnect hoses before a hard freeze, cover the faucet with an insulated dome and let an indoor tap drip slowly on a very cold night. Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls.

Do it yourself or call a plumber

A drip at the spout is a fair do it yourself job if you shut off the supply first and bring the old washer to the store. A leak inside the wall is not. If a faucet will not shut off, the shutoff valve inside the house may also be stuck, and forcing it can break it.

Preventing the next failure

Install a frost proof bib on the next replacement. Add a vacuum breaker so hose water cannot siphon back into the house. Keep hoses off the faucet in winter. A small habit saves a large repair.

Check the faucet each spring by turning it on and looking inside for a drip or a damp stain. A few minutes of attention finds a leak while it is small.
