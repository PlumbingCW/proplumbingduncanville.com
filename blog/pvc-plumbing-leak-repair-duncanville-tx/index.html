PVC Plumbing Leak Repair in Duncanville, TX

Why PVC joints leak and what a lasting repair looks like.

Call (972) 903-8224

PVC is the white or cream colored plastic pipe that carries drain, waste and vent water in most homes built or repaired in recent decades. It is also used for cold water supply in some systems and for irrigation lines in yards. PVC is tough and inexpensive, and when it leaks, the cause is usually a joint, not the pipe wall.

This guide explains where you find PVC in a Duncanville home, why PVC leaks happen, what a lasting repair looks like and what to avoid. It also covers a point many people miss, which is that a quick wrap or tape fix is a temporary measure.

Where PVC is used around the house

  • Drain lines under sinks, which are the white plastic pipes you can see in a cabinet
  • Main drain and branch drain lines in newer homes and in repaired sections
  • Vent pipes that rise through the roof
  • Irrigation lines in the yard
  • Cold water supply lines to a hose bib or a water softener

Hot water lines are not usually PVC. Standard PVC is not rated for hot water. Hot lines are copper, PEX or CPVC, a related plastic made for heat. If you are not sure what a pipe is, ask a plumber before you cut or cement it.

Why PVC leaks

The joints are where the trouble starts. PVC pieces are joined with primer and solvent cement, which fuses the plastic. If the joint was not cleaned, primed, fully inserted and held in place, it may seep for years before it fails. Movement from the ground, a heavy load on the pipe or a strike with a shovel can open a joint.

Sunlight makes outdoor PVC brittle over time. Freezing can crack a pipe that holds water. Threaded fittings leak when they are overtightened or when sealant is wrong. A drain pipe that slopes the wrong way can hold water at a joint and leak slowly.

A lasting repair, step by step

  1. 01Shut off and drainClose the water, open a low faucet and let the pipe drain. Cement will not bond on a wet pipe.
  2. 02Cut out the bad sectionCut square on both sides of the damage with a PVC cutter or a fine toothed saw.
  3. 03Clean and primeDeburr the ends, dry fit the parts, then clean and prime the pipe and fittings.
  4. 04Cement and cureApply cement, push together with a quarter turn, hold for several seconds and respect the cure time before pressure.

Rubber couplings with steel bands are a useful fix when you cannot twist a pipe together. They are not a replacement for a proper joint in a pressurized line, and they should not be buried unless rated for it.

Temporary fixes and what they are good for

Self fusing silicone tape, epoxy putty and clamp style repair sleeves slow a leak. They are useful at 2 a.m. when you need to limit damage. They are not a repair. Pressure and temperature change, and a patch over a failed joint can let go suddenly.

If you use a temporary patch, plan a proper fix within days, and keep the main valve handy. If the leak is in a wall or a ceiling, shut the water off and call a plumber, since you cannot see how bad the damage is.

When the leak is inside a wall or under the floor

Hidden PVC leaks are harder. A drain leak does not spray under pressure, so the water seeps slowly and shows up as a stain, a smell or a damp baseboard. A supply leak shows up on the meter. We use moisture meters, thermal cameras and sometimes a camera in the drain line to find the source before opening anything.

See leak detection for how we narrow the location. If the leak is in a drain below the slab, a camera inspection shows cracks and offsets that are invisible from above.

Keep a few spare couplings, a can of primer, a can of cement and a small saw in the garage. When a PVC joint gives way on a Sunday, having the right parts decides whether the repair takes an hour or waits for the store to open.

Check the dates on the primer and the cement, because both go bad when they thicken. If the cement is stringy, buy a fresh can.

Permits, codes and license checks in Duncanville

The City of Duncanville adopted the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC). Small repairs such as replacing a section of drain may not need a permit, but rules vary, so ask Permit and Inspection Services at (972) 780-5000.

Before you hire anyone for a larger repair, ask for the Responsible Master Plumber license number and look it up on tsbpe.texas.gov. The license number begins with the letter M.

Prevent the next leak

Support horizontal pipe with hangers, keep outdoor PVC out of direct sun when possible and protect it from mowers. Do not use PVC for a hot water line. Never glue a pipe that is wet or dirty. When in doubt, ask. A leak that was caused by a bad joint tends to repeat if the new joint is made the same way.
