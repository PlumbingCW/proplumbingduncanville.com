Faucet Leak Repair in Duncanville, TX

Causes of drips, a simple DIY approach, and when to call a plumber.

Call (972) 903-8224

A leaking faucet is more than an annoyance. A drip a second wastes water and shows up on the bill. A leak under the sink can rot the cabinet and grow mold before anyone notices. Most faucet leaks are easy to understand, because a faucet has few parts, and many are simple to fix.

This guide covers the causes of faucet leaks, how to find which part has failed, what a careful homeowner can fix and when a plumber is the better choice. It is written for Duncanville homes, where older fixtures and worn shutoff valves are common.

Where the water is coming from

The location of the leak tells you the part. A drip from the spout when the faucet is off points to a worn cartridge, washer or seat. Water around the base of the handle points to a worn O ring or a loose packing nut. Water under the sink points to a supply line, a stop valve or the faucet body.

Dry the area, place a piece of dry paper towel and watch. The first wet spot is your clue. Do not skip this step, because replacing the wrong part wastes an afternoon.

A basic repair for a cartridge faucet

  1. 01Shut off waterClose the stop valves under the sink. Open the faucet to release pressure.
  2. 02Remove the handlePry off the cap, remove the screw and lift the handle off. Keep parts in order.
  3. 03Pull the cartridgeRemove the retaining clip or nut and pull the cartridge straight out.
  4. 04Replace and testInstall an identical cartridge, reassemble, open the stops slowly and check for drips.

Take the old cartridge to the store so the new one matches. Cartridges look alike but are not interchangeable between brands. If you cannot find an exact match, a new faucet may be simpler.

Hard water and scale

Mineral scale can keep a valve from sealing and clog the aerator. According to the 2022 Dallas Water Utilities report, Duncanville's water is treated surface water, and the report we reviewed gives no hardness figure, so test your tap if you see white crust on fixtures. Soaking an aerator in vinegar removes light scale and costs almost nothing.

If the scale is heavy and returns, a softener may help. See water softener and filtration for how to test first.

When to call a plumber

  • The stop valves will not close
  • The leak is under the sink and you cannot see the source
  • The faucet is old and parts are not available
  • The faucet body is corroded or cracked
  • Water is damaging the cabinet or floor
  • You are not comfortable working with water lines

A stop valve that will not close is the common surprise. It has probably not been turned in years. Forcing it can break the stem and flood the cabinet. A plumber can replace it, and that gives you a working shutoff for the next time.

Supply lines and shutoff valves

A faucet repair is a good time to look at the parts that rarely get attention. The braided supply lines under the sink age, and a line that is more than about a decade old is worth replacing when the faucet is apart.

The stop valves, the small oval handles on the wall or the floor of the cabinet, should turn smoothly and shut off completely. If one is crusted or stiff, replace it now rather than during the next leak.

Take a photograph of the setup before you disconnect anything, and label the hot and cold lines with a strip of tape. A clear picture saves you from reversing a connection.

Tools and parts worth having

A small kit covers most faucet jobs. You need an adjustable wrench, a screwdriver set, a hex key set, a basin wrench for nuts that hide behind the sink, plumber's grease, a few spare O rings and a flashlight.

Bring the old cartridge or washer to the store so the new one matches. Keep the shutoff valves closed while you work, place a towel in the cabinet and a bowl under the connections, and take a photo before you disassemble anything.

Plumber's tape on threaded joints and a light film of grease on rubber parts make reassembly easier. Stop tightening when the joint is snug. Overtightening cracks plastic nuts and flattens washers, and it turns a simple repair into a larger one.

Repair or replace

A repair is the right move when the faucet is in good shape and parts exist. A replacement is the right move when the finish has worn off, the body is corroded or the parts are discontinued. A new faucet is not expensive compared with repeated repairs, and a good one lasts for years.
