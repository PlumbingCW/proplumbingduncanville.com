Titan N120 Tankless Water Heater in Duncanville, TX

What the Titan N120 electric tankless heater needs and what it can realistically run.

Call (972) 903-8224

The Titan N120 is an electric tankless water heater sold by Niagara Industries under the Titan name. Retail listings describe the N-120 as an 11.8 kilowatt, 220 volt unit made for a whole house or a smaller home with modest demand. People search for it because it is compact, does not need a gas line or a vent and costs less than many gas tankless units.

This guide helps Duncanville homeowners decide whether the model fits. It explains what the unit needs from the house, what it can reasonably run, where it struggles and what to ask an installer. We install tankless units, but this is not a sales page for one brand.

What the unit is

The N120 heats water with electric elements. There is no burner and no flue, so it can be mounted indoors without a vent. When a tap opens, a sensor turns the elements on. When the tap closes, they shut off. The Titan line uses electronic controls to adjust the heat to the flow.

Because it is electric, it avoids the gas line, the vent and the condensate drain that a gas tankless needs. In exchange, it needs a large block of electric power, and that is the main constraint in an existing house.

The electrical question comes first

Electric heaters are rated in kilowatts. Current is watts divided by volts. At 11,800 watts and 240 volts, the unit draws about 49 amps while it is heating. That is the arithmetic of the rating, and it means a dedicated circuit with heavy wire and a breaker sized to the manufacturer's instructions.

Many older panels have little spare capacity. A licensed electrician must confirm that the panel can carry the load alongside an air conditioner, an oven and a dryer. If it cannot, a panel or service upgrade may be needed, and that cost can change the decision.

What it can realistically run

An 11.8 kilowatt electric unit can raise a limited flow of water by a set number of degrees. In warm months, it may handle one shower at a time. In cold months, with colder inlet water, the same unit delivers less hot water at the set temperature. Two showers at once is a stretch.

That makes the N120 a better fit for a small house, a guest suite, a granny flat or a point of use setting than for a large family home. For a large household, a gas tankless or a tank may fit better. Be honest about your peak use before you buy.

Reading the label and the manual

Retail listings are a starting point, not a specification. The label on the exact unit, and the installation manual that ships with it, are the documents that decide the breaker size, the wire gauge, the clearances and the minimum flow. Model names change, and a listing may describe an older or a newer version than the one in the box.

Before you buy, find the manual online, read the electrical section and send it to your installer and your electrician. Ask them to confirm in writing that the house can support the unit. A ten minute review before purchase can save a return and a second trip.

Point of use versus whole house

A small electric unit is often used at the point of use, close to a single bathroom or a kitchen, to deliver hot water without a long wait. In that role it can be an excellent choice.

As the only heater in a large house, it is a harder fit. If you want to use it as a point of use unit, plan the cold supply, the circuit and the location together so the install is neat and serviceable.

A final thought on expectations. An electric tankless heater is a good product in the right setting, and an unhappy one in the wrong setting. If you want a small unit near one bathroom, it can serve you well for years.

If you want to power the whole house in winter, size with care and ask your installer for the numbers. An honest installer would rather lose the sale than leave you with cold showers in January.

Water and permits in Duncanville

Duncanville buys water from the City of Dallas. The 2022 report we reviewed gives no hardness figure, so test your tap before you set a flush schedule. For water quality questions, call Public Works at (972) 780-4900.

Wiring for the unit falls under the National Electrical Code (NEC), and permit rules vary, since electrical and plumbing work may have separate requirements. Call Permit and Inspection Services at (972) 780-5000 before you begin. Verify any plumber you hire at tsbpe.texas.gov.

When another unit is a better pick

  • The house has a gas line and a vent, and you need whole house output
  • The panel is small and an upgrade is not planned
  • A large family uses several showers at once
  • You want the lowest upfront cost, in which case a tank may win
