How to Repair a Slab Leak in Duncanville, TX

What a slab leak repair day looks like, from tools to patching the floor.

Call (972) 903-8224

Another guide on this site explains how to recognize a leaking slab. This one assumes you know the leak is there and walks through the repair. It covers what happens on repair day, the three main repair paths, what homeowners prepare and what to expect afterward.

It is written for Duncanville homeowners who want to understand the steps before they agree to a price. A repair done well is quiet and quick. A repair done poorly leaves a patch that cracks, a floor that never matches or a leak that returns.

Before the repair: confirm and plan

A repair starts with a located leak. The plumber should have marked the spot using acoustic or thermal tools and explained which line it is. You should know the line type, the likely cause and the options. Ask for the written scope and the price before work starts.

Plan the house too. The water will be off during the repair. Clear furniture from the area. Move valuables and photographs. If the slab will be opened under a kitchen cabinet or tile, ask how the cabinet or tile is handled.

Path one: spot repair through the slab

  1. 01Mark and protectThe plumber marks the cut lines, covers floors and sets up dust control.
  2. 02Cut and breakA concrete saw cuts a small opening, and the concrete is broken out to expose the pipe.
  3. 03Repair the pipeThe damaged section is cut out and replaced with new pipe or a proper coupling, then pressure tested.
  4. 04Backfill and patchThe hole is backfilled, concrete is patched and the surface is leveled for new flooring.

The opening is small, often a square under two feet on each side. The mess is real, so expect noise and dust. A good crew contains it, and a good plan puts the opening where the pipe is, not where it is convenient.

Path two: reroute above the slab

A reroute abandons the leaking line and runs new pipe through the attic and down walls to the fixtures. It avoids concrete and flooring completely, except where a pipe emerges. The crew opens small sections of drywall and patches them.

It makes sense when a line is old and likely to leak again or when it passes under tile that is costly to replace. The old pipe stays in the slab, capped or abandoned in place. The new lines are accessible for future repairs, which is the main long term benefit.

Path three: whole house repipe

If the house has had several leaks, the pipe is probably failing. A repipe replaces all supply lines in one project. It costs more, but ends the leak cycle and often includes a new pressure regulator and shutoff valves. We recommend it when a pipe shows signs of general corrosion, not after a single failure.

Testing and restoration

  1. A pressure test confirms that the new section or the whole system holds.
  2. The water is restored slowly and air is purged from the lines.
  3. The meter is checked with every fixture off to confirm it stays still.
  4. The slab is patched with concrete, and a cure time is respected.
  5. Flooring is repaired or replaced by the plumber or another contractor, as agreed.

Ask who handles the floor. Many plumbers repair concrete but not tile or carpet. If a pattern or a color must match, plan for it. A reroute often leaves floors untouched.

How to prepare your home for repair day

  • Clear a path from the door to the work area
  • Move furniture, rugs and breakable items out of the room
  • Fill containers with water for drinking and flushing, since the supply will be off for part of the day
  • Secure pets in another room, because saws and dust are hard on them
  • Take photographs of the floor and walls before work starts
  • Keep the leak report and the written scope where the crew can see them

A prepared house makes the repair faster and the cleanup shorter. It also protects you in a dispute, because the photographs show the starting condition.

After the repair, ask the plumber to show you the old pipe. Seeing the failed section tells you whether the cause was a pinhole, a worn bend or a break, and it helps you judge whether other lines are likely to fail the same way.

Keep the report, the photographs and the invoice together in one folder. They are useful at resale, and they matter if a claim is ever disputed.

Duncanville rules and records

Permit rules vary by project, and cutting a slab may need a permit. Ask Permit and Inspection Services at (972) 780-5000. The city adopted the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC).

If the repair is disputed, the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners has a complaint page at tsbpe.texas.gov. The board says it does not regulate prices, so a written estimate matters.

Questions to ask before you agree

  • Which line is leaking, and how was it confirmed?
  • What are the options, and what does each cost in writing?
  • Who patches the slab and the floor?
  • Is there a warranty on the repair and on the patch?
  • What happens if another leak appears on the same line?

A repair is a good time to check other parts of the system, such as the water heater connections and the pressure regulator. High pressure causes leaks. Our slab leak repair page describes how we handle these jobs, and we serve Cedar Hill and Grand Prairie homeowners with the same repairs.
