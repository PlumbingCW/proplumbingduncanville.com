Tankless Water Heater Guide for Duncanville, TX Homes

Sizing, fuel, upgrades and care for a tankless water heater in a Texas home.

Call (972) 903-8224

Tankless water heaters promise endless hot water and a smaller footprint. Many people search for them when the old tank begins to fail. A tankless unit is not right for every house, and a wrong choice can leave you with cold showers or a bill for upgrades you did not expect.

This guide is for Duncanville homeowners who want to decide with the facts. It covers how a tankless heater works, how to size one, what the house needs, what care it takes and what a homeowner should ask. Some searches add the word Centennial to this topic. That is a place name in another state, so we have focused on what matters for a North Texas home.

How tankless works

A tankless heater has a heat exchanger and a burner or electric elements, but no storage tank. When a hot tap opens, a flow sensor starts the heater. Water passes through the exchanger and leaves hot. When the tap closes, the heater stops.

There is no tank to run out of hot water, but there is a maximum flow. If you ask for more hot water than the unit can heat at once, the temperature drops. Sizing is the heart of the decision.

Sizing: how much hot water do you use at once

  1. List the hot water fixtures you might run at the same time, such as two showers, a dishwasher and a laundry.
  2. Add the flow rate of each fixture. A shower is often a couple of gallons per minute, and a kitchen sink a little more than one.
  3. Estimate the temperature rise. Inlet water in a Texas winter is colder than in summer, and the unit must raise it to the set point.
  4. Choose a unit whose output covers the peak at the winter rise, not the summer one.

A professional does this with real numbers. An undersized unit will disappoint you every winter. An oversized unit costs more up front and may need a bigger gas line than you have.

What a Duncanville house usually needs

Duncanville grew from 3,774 residents in 1960 to 27,781 in 1980, and many homes of that era were built with a gas line and a panel sized for a 40 or 50 gallon tank. Swapping to tankless often means a larger gas line, a new vent route and a wall location, or a panel upgrade for electric.

These upgrades are where tankless gets expensive. A good plumber tells you the total scope before you decide. In some homes, a high efficiency tank or a hybrid unit is the better value. We say so when it is true.

Water quality and care

Duncanville buys treated surface water from Dallas. The 2022 report we reviewed shows chloramine averaging 2.16 parts per million and no hardness figure. A tankless heat exchanger collects scale when water is hard, and it narrows over time. Test your tap and set a flush schedule from what you learn.

A yearly flush with a mild acid solution through the service valves is a common recommendation. Isolation valves make this easy, so ask your installer to include them.

A realistic day with a tankless heater

Picture a Tuesday morning with a teenager in the shower, a dishwasher running and someone washing hands at the kitchen sink. A tank heater serves all three from stored hot water until the tank runs low.

A tankless heater serves all three only if its output covers the combined flow at the winter temperature rise. That is the daily test, and it is why peak demand matters more than the average.

Tankless heaters also behave differently at very low flow. A tap opened just a little may not trigger the sensor, and you can get a short burst of cold water between two uses.

Many modern units reduce this with better sensors, and a small recirculation loop can help in a long house. Ask the installer how the model you are considering handles low flow, because it shapes how the heater feels to live with.

Cost over time, not just the first bill

A tankless heater avoids the standby loss of keeping a tank hot all day, and it can last longer than a tank when it is flushed on schedule. Against that, it costs more up front and may need electrical or gas upgrades. Whether it pays back depends on how much hot water you use, what you pay for energy and how long you stay in the house.

We do not quote a payback figure, because it depends on numbers only you have. Ask for the total installed scope in writing and compare it with a tank quote line by line.

Questions to ask any installer

  • What is the unit's output at the winter temperature rise?
  • What upgrades does my house need, and what do they cost?
  • Are isolation valves and a condensate neutralizer included?
  • What is the flush schedule and who does it?
  • What does the warranty cover?
  • What is the permit plan?

If the answers are clear, tankless can be a good fit. If you want to compare, our tankless water heater page and our water heater installation page show both approaches. Households in Grand Prairie and DeSoto ask the same questions.
