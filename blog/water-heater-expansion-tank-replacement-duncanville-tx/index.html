Water Heater Expansion Tank Replacement in Duncanville, TX

What an expansion tank does and how to tell that yours has failed.

Call (972) 903-8224

A thermal expansion tank is a small tank attached to the cold water line near a water heater. Its job is to absorb the extra volume created when water is heated. Without one, a closed plumbing system builds pressure every time the heater runs, and that pressure shows up as dripping relief valves, noisy pipes and leaks.

This guide is for Duncanville homeowners who see a dripping valve, noisy pipes or a heater that seems to leak and who want to understand the cause. It explains why expansion tanks matter, how to check one and what replacement involves.

Why heated water needs somewhere to go

Water expands when heated. A small amount of expansion used to flow back into the city main when the plumbing system was open. Many homes now have a check valve or a pressure reducing valve at the meter or the main that prevents backflow. That makes the system closed, and the expansion has nowhere to go.

In a closed system, the pressure rises sharply as the heater runs. A temperature and pressure relief valve on the tank opens to release the excess, which is why it drips. The expansion tank gives the water room, with a cushion of air on one side of a rubber diaphragm.

Signs of a failed expansion tank

  • The relief valve drips or discharges after a heating cycle
  • Pressure at a tap or hose bib jumps at night or after a shower
  • Banging pipes or water hammer when a fixture shuts off
  • A tank that is heavy and cold when tapped, which suggests it is waterlogged
  • Water at the schrader valve on the tank when it is pressed
  • Faucets or toilet fill valves that wear out too fast

A healthy tank is half air and half water. Tap it. The top should sound hollow. Press the air valve gently. If water comes out, the diaphragm has failed. Do this with the water off and the pressure released.

How replacement works

  1. 01Test the pressureA gauge on a hose bib shows the static pressure. Sustained readings above the safe range need attention.
  2. 02Shut and drainWe close the cold supply, relieve pressure and drain the line to the tank.
  3. 03Replace the tankThe failed tank is unscrewed from its tee, and a new tank is installed and supported.
  4. 04Set the airThe air charge is set to match the house water pressure before the tank is put into service.

The size of the tank depends on the heater, and the air precharge must match the line pressure. A tank charged to the wrong pressure will not absorb expansion. That setting is the step many homeowners miss.

Is it the tank or the regulator

A tank is not the only cause of high pressure. Pressure from the street can run high, and a failing pressure regulator lets it through. A plumber can measure the pressure at the house and decide. If the regulator is failing, a new tank will not solve it.

If you need more pressure data, the City of Duncanville Public Works at (972) 780-4900 can answer questions about water service. Water bought from Dallas arrives in the city main, and what happens after the meter is the homeowner's system.

How to check pressure yourself

A simple pressure gauge that threads onto a hose bib shows the static pressure in your house. Close all fixtures, attach the gauge, and read it. Check it again late at night when demand in the area is low, because pressure can rise when neighbors use less water.

If the reading climbs well beyond the normal household range, the cause is a regulator that is letting pressure creep or heat that is expanding trapped water.

Take a photograph of the gauge and give it to the plumber. A recorded reading tells us more than a description, and it shows whether the problem is in the house or in the supply.

Sizing and mounting

Expansion tanks are sized to the heater and the system. A tank that is too small fills quickly and stops helping. A tank must be mounted so its weight is supported, and it should hang from a sturdy fitting or a bracket, not from a soft copper line. The air valve must be accessible so the precharge can be checked in future years.

If you are replacing a water heater soon, ask the installer to check the expansion tank at the same visit. A failed tank on an old heater will damage the new one in the same way, and the cost of adding or replacing a tank at the time of installation is small compared with the cost of a second visit.

Ask what size the tank is and where the air valve is so you can check the charge in future years.

What to do and safety note for the relief valve

The relief valve is a safety device. Never plug it, cap it or run the discharge into a sealed pipe. If it drips, fix the cause. The valve should have a discharge pipe that runs downward and ends in a safe place. A dripping valve that is ignored can wear until it fails to open when it matters.
