Trenchless Sewer Line Repair in Duncanville, TX

How lining and pipe bursting work, and when a dig is still the better choice.

Call (972) 903-8224

Trenchless sewer repair is a way to fix a buried sewer pipe without digging a trench along its whole length. For a homeowner it can mean keeping a mature lawn, a driveway or a patio.

For the plumber it means working from two small access points instead of one long open cut. The method is real and widely used, but it is not magic, and it suits some pipes better than others.

This guide explains the two main trenchless methods, what a camera inspection tells you before you decide, and what to ask a contractor. It is written for homeowners in Duncanville, where many houses date from the 1960s through the 1980s and many sewer laterals are cast iron or clay.

What trenchless repair actually means

The word trenchless covers several techniques. In every case, a plumber reaches the pipe through a small hole, often at the cleanout and at the point where the line meets the street connection. The old pipe stays in the ground, or it is broken up in place, and a new pipe takes over its job.

The two common types are pipe lining and pipe bursting. They solve different problems, and the choice is driven by the condition of the pipe, not by what is cheaper or newer.

MethodHow it worksNeeds
Cured in place liningA resin soaked felt tube is pulled into the old pipe, inflated and cured to form a new pipe inside the old oneA pipe that is clear, fairly round and joined without major gaps
Pipe burstingA cone shaped head pulls through the old pipe, breaking it outward while pulling new pipe behind itEntry and exit pits, and room around the line
Spot repairA short section is dug and replacedOnly one place failed and the rest of the pipe is sound

Why the camera comes first

A lining is only as good as the pipe it lines. A tube cannot span a dropped joint or a collapsed section, and it will not cure properly in a pipe that is full of roots or standing water.

That is why a sewer camera inspection comes before any trenchless quote. The video shows cracks, offsets, roots and bellies, and it records the distance to each so the plumber can plan access points.

If a company quotes a trenchless repair by phone and has never seen the pipe, treat the number with caution. A good quote starts from video.

When trenchless works well, and when it does not

Lining suits a lateral that has cracks, small root intrusions and open joints but still holds its shape. Pipe bursting suits a lateral that is badly cracked or undersized, where a full replacement is needed but you want to avoid a long trench. Both methods have limits.

A pipe with a bad belly, where it sags and holds water, will not drain better after lining because the lining follows the sag. A pipe with a collapse needs a dig. A line that has multiple direction changes can be hard to burst. A plumber who recommends trenchless for every case is not looking at the pipe.

  • Good candidates: cracked cast iron or clay with intact shape
  • Good candidates: root intrusion at joints after the roots are cut out
  • Poor candidates: collapsed or heavily offset pipe
  • Poor candidates: bellies that hold standing water
  • Poor candidates: lines with many fittings and sharp turns

Why Duncanville laterals fail

Duncanville grew from 841 residents in 1950 to 35,008 in 1990, so the city has a large stock of homes built when cast iron and clay laterals were standard. Cast iron scales from the inside and rots from the outside. Clay joints loosen and admit roots.

The ground adds to the stress. Clay soils of the type described in the USDA Houston Black series, common in the Dallas region, form cracks 0.5 to 4 inches wide in dry months and swell when wet.

A rigid pipe in that kind of soil sees repeated movement, and the weakest joint gives first. A new liner cannot stop the ground from moving, but it can bridge a cracked joint and keep roots out.

What to ask a contractor

  1. Can I see the camera video of my pipe, and where is the problem?
  2. Why is this method the right one for this pipe, and what would you choose if it were not available?
  3. What is the warranty, and does it cover the full length?
  4. Is a permit needed, and who pulls it?
  5. What happens to the cleanout and the street connection after the work?
  6. What is the written price, and what is excluded?

Ask for a written scope. Texas does not regulate what plumbers charge, so a clear written price is your protection. The cost depends on the length, the depth, the pipe diameter, the number of access points and the method. We do not publish dollar ranges because the pipe decides the number.

Duncanville permits and license checks

Duncanville adopted the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC). Permit and Inspection Services at 203 E. Wheatland Road, (972) 780-5000, is open Monday through Friday from 8 to 5 and can say what a sewer repair requires. Contractors register through the Citizen Access Portal.

To check a plumber, ask for the Responsible Master Plumber license number. It begins with the letter M and can be looked up on the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners site at tsbpe.texas.gov.

After the work

A repaired line should be tested with a camera after the work and a water test through the house. Keep the final video. Ask how to maintain it, since a lined pipe still needs to be kept free of grease and wipes, and a root problem upstream or downstream can return.

If your line is fine but slow, a cleaning may be all you need. See sewer line cleaning for what that involves. If a pipe is clearly broken, sewer line repair explains the options we offer.
