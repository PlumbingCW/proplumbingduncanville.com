Emergency plumber, Duncanville TX

An emergency plumber who answers at any hour

Plumbing does not fail on a schedule. Pipes burst in the middle of the night, a main line backs up during Sunday dinner and a water heater lets go before a holiday morning. Duncanville Pro Plumbing runs a 24/7 emergency line for Duncanville and the towns around it, and every call goes to dispatch that can send a licensed plumber as soon as one is free.

When you call, we ask three things: what is happening, where the water is going and whether anyone is at risk. Those answers decide who comes and what goes on the truck. If water is spreading, we talk you through the main valve first. We would rather you stop the damage in two minutes than wait for us in a flooded kitchen.

We are an emergency plumber with a regular day schedule too. Same day appointments are available when the calendar allows, and planned jobs such as a new water heater, a repipe or a camera inspection are scheduled at a time that suits you. Whichever way you reach us, the price comes before the work.

Local knowledge

Plumbing in a city that grew in a hurry

According to the Handbook of Texas, Duncanville was incorporated on August 2, 1947, and it grew fast. The census counted 841 people in 1950, 14,105 in 1970 and 35,008 in 1990, and the city reported 40,706 in 2020.

Commerce grew along US 67, and the city adopted a home rule charter in 1962. That history is why so many Duncanville houses were built in the same few decades, with similar cast iron drains, copper supply lines and original water heaters that are now due for replacement.

The ground adds its own stress. The USDA describes Houston Black clay, a Blackland Prairie soil, as cracking 0.5 to 4 inches wide when dry and swelling when wet.

Clay soils of this type are common in the Dallas region, and they move slabs, pipes and sewer joints over the years. When we trace a slab leak or a cracked lateral near Armstrong Park, along Main Street or off Camp Wisdom Road, that movement is often part of the story.

The water comes from the City of Dallas. Dallas Water Utilities draws from the Elm Fork of the Trinity River and several lakes, and treats it with chloramine, which averaged 2.16 parts per million in its 2022 report.

The report gives no hardness figure, so we ask homeowners to test their tap before buying a softener. For city utility questions, Public Works is at (972) 780-4900, and the after hours water emergency line is (972) 780-4959.

Families in Forest Hills, near the Recreation Center and the Public Library, and along I-20 and US 67 call us for the same handful of problems: drains that clog again, hot water that runs out, hidden leaks and sewer lines that have reached the end of their life. Our leak detection, drain cleaning and water heater repair pages go into each one.
