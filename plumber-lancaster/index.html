Backups and old pipe: the problem Lancaster calls about first

A sewage backup in an older house is the call that sends people to the phone at midnight. Lancaster has some of the oldest housing and streets in our service area, and old lines are the most likely to fail without warning. If sewage is coming up in a tub or floor drain, stop using water, keep people out of the area and call our 24/7 line.

Our dispatch covers Lancaster every day, including nights and holidays, and sends a plumber as soon as one is free. We start with the cause: a clog, a bellied pipe, roots or a broken line. Each has a different fix, and a camera look is often the fastest way to tell them apart.

A city with real age

According to the Handbook of Texas, Lancaster was incorporated on May 22, 1887. Its population was 1,632 in 1950 and 7,501 in 1960, so a good share of the town was built in the decades when cast iron and clay sewer laterals were standard. The Randlett House, built in 1896, and the Rawlins Homestead, dating to 1855, still stand as reminders of how long people have lived here.

Old housing stock means old pipe. Clay lines let roots in at the joints, cast iron scales and thins, and galvanized supply pipe closes down with rust. A house with original plumbing may need targeted repairs now and a repipe later, and we lay out that path honestly.
