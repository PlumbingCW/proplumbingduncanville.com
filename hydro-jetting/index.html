Emergency Hydro Jetting in Duncanville, TX

When a cable only buys a few weeks, high pressure water scours the whole pipe wall and ends the repeat backups.

Call (972) 903-8224

When a sewer line backs up and a cable only buys a few weeks, hydro jetting is the next step. We take emergency calls on the 24/7 line, and a backup that has already reached the floor is exactly the case where waiting makes cleanup worse. While you wait, stop using water, shut off the dishwasher and washer, and keep people out of any wet area.

Hydro jetting sends water through a hose at high pressure and out of a nozzle that sprays backward and forward at once. The forward jets break up the blockage and the rear jets pull the hose along while scouring the pipe wall. The result is a clean bore instead of a narrow hole.

Sewer backup: what to do first

  1. Stop all water use, including flushing, laundry and the dishwasher.
  2. Open the cleanout cap outside only if you know where it is and it is safe to do so. Standing sewage can release gas.
  3. Keep children, pets and bare feet away from any water on the floor.
  4. Call the 24/7 line and tell dispatch which fixtures are affected.

A backup is a health matter as well as a plumbing one. Sewage on porous flooring, carpet or drywall should be treated as contaminated, and the sooner a line is cleared the less area has to be cleaned afterward.

When jetting is the right tool

A cable is a good tool for a single mass of hair or a wad of paper. It is a poor tool for a pipe whose whole wall is coated. Grease from decades of kitchen use, mineral scale in older cast iron and fine roots clinging to joints all leave the pipe narrower than it should be, and a cable passes through the middle without touching them.

Jetting is the fix when a line has been snaked repeatedly, when a camera shows heavy build up, or when a restaurant or a multi unit property cannot afford another backup. It is also used after root cutting to wash the debris out so the roots have nothing to restart from.

What happens during a jetting visit

  1. 01Inspect firstWe run a camera through the line when possible, so we know the pipe will survive the pressure.
  2. 02Set the accessThe hose enters at a cleanout. We match the nozzle to the pipe size and the type of blockage.
  3. 03Jet in passesThe hose moves forward and back in passes until the water running out is clear of debris.
  4. 04Re-inspectA second camera pass shows the finished pipe, and you see the difference for yourself.

What decides the price of hydro jetting

Jetting needs a truck mounted or trailer unit and more water than snaking, so the price reflects equipment and time.

  • Line length and sizeA 40 foot branch is a short job. A long run to the street takes longer and uses more water.
  • Amount of build upHeavy grease or scale needs several passes before the water runs clear.
  • Camera workA before and after camera pass adds time but removes guesswork.
  • AccessA proper cleanout makes jetting quick. Without one, the plumber has to find another way into the line.

We give a clear price before work starts, and we explain when a cable would do the job for less.

When we recommend against jetting

Pressure is a tool, not a cure. A pipe that is cracked, offset or collapsed can be damaged further by jetting, which is why we look with a camera first.

Older clay and thin walled cast iron need a lower pressure setting, and some failing pipes are better handled by a lining or a replacement. A plumber who offers jetting for every clog is selling a machine. We would rather tell you what the camera shows.
