Repiping in Duncanville, TX

When pinhole leaks and rusty water keep returning, replacing the supply lines ends the cycle of repairs.

Call (972) 903-8224

Repiping replaces the water supply pipes in a house, either in part or throughout. It is a planned project that usually follows a series of leaks, low water pressure, discolored water or an old pipe material that is known to fail. The goal is to end the cycle of repairs by replacing the problem pipe with a new one that will serve for decades.

The work is disruptive but manageable when well planned. A crew can usually repipe a typical home in a few days, and the water is shut off for part of each day. We walk the house with you first so you know where walls will be opened and how they will be patched.

Signs the pipes are done

  • Pinhole leaks that appear in more than one place
  • Low water pressure that gets worse with time
  • Brown or rusty water when you first open a tap
  • Visible green corrosion or white mineral crusting on copper
  • Gray plastic pipe known as polybutylene, used in some homes from the late 1970s to the mid 1990s
  • Frequent repairs on galvanized steel lines

A single leak is a repair. Repeated leaks in different spots are a pattern. Before a whole house repipe, we use leak detection to confirm the cause, since sometimes high pressure or a failed regulator is responsible and a cheaper fix is available.

PEX versus copper

MaterialStrengthsTrade-offs
PEXFlexible, fewer fittings, easier to route through finished walls, resists freezing damage better than rigid pipeMust be protected from sunlight and some chemicals, joins use specific fittings
CopperLong record, strong, handles heat and sunlightCosts more, more joints, can suffer from aggressive water or pinholes
CPVCLower cost, common for cold and hot lines in some homesCan become brittle with age and heat

We explain each option. For a typical home, PEX is a common choice because the flexible tubing runs through walls with less demolition. Copper remains a good choice for exposed runs and customers who prefer it. Which is better depends on the house and the budget.

How a repipe is done

  1. 01SurveyWe map every fixture and decide where the new lines will run, aiming to avoid unneeded wall openings.
  2. 02Rough inNew lines are installed alongside the old, with new shutoff valves and a pressure regulator where needed.
  3. 03Switch over and testWe connect fixtures, flush the lines and pressure test before closing walls.
  4. 04Patch and cleanOpenings are patched to a paintable surface, and the old pipe is removed or abandoned in place as agreed.

What decides the price of a repipe

Repiping is priced to the house. You get a clear written scope and price before work starts.

  • Size of the houseMore bathrooms and fixtures mean more pipe and more connections.
  • MaterialPEX, copper and CPVC are priced differently.
  • AccessOpen attics and crawl spaces are quicker. A slab foundation and finished walls take longer.
  • PatchingDrywall and paint can be included or handled by your own contractor.

Get the written scope, the material brand and the warranty terms before you agree.

Slab homes and routing

Many North Texas houses sit on a concrete slab, so supply lines often run through the attic and down interior walls rather than under the floor. Rerouting through the attic lets us avoid breaking the slab. If an existing line runs under the slab and is leaking, rerouting around the leak is often cheaper than a slab repair, which is why slab leak repair and repiping are linked.

Repiping in Duncanville homes

The City of Duncanville completed a water service line survey on November 13, 2024, with extra attention on homes built before 1988, and describes its service lines as primarily non lead or unknown. The service line runs from the meter to your house, and a repipe usually starts at the house side of that connection. To ask about your service line, call Public Works at (972) 780-4900.

Permit rules vary, and Permit and Inspection Services at (972) 780-5000 can tell you what a repipe requires. Duncanville adopted the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC).

A repipe is often paired with a new water heater or a plumbing installation for fixtures. We repipe homes in Lancaster and Red Oak as well.

Repiping projects are scheduled in 75116, 75137 and 75138 and in older Lancaster neighborhoods in ZIP 75134.
