DeSoto was incorporated in 1949 and grew quickly. According to the Handbook of Texas, the census counts show 1,969 people in 1960, 6,617 in 1970, 15,538 in 1980 and 56,145 in 2020. That is a city of many ages of housing, and each age has its own typical plumbing trouble.

Housing eras in DeSoto and the plumbing that goes with them

The jump from 6,617 residents in 1970 to 15,538 in 1980 means a large share of DeSoto homes date from the 1970s. Houses of that era commonly have copper supply lines and cast iron or early plastic drains. Cast iron scales and cracks with age, and drains that were fine in the 1990s now clog more easily. Water heaters in these homes are often on their second or third tank.

Growth continued after 1980, so many newer subdivisions have PEX or CPVC supply and PVC drains. Those materials resist corrosion but have their own issues, such as a failed fitting or a poorly bedded drain that holds water. Knowing the era of your house helps a plumber guess before arriving.

The Trinity River Authority handles wastewater treatment for DeSoto, which matters when sewage backs up. A blockage inside your own line is yours to fix. If a neighbor on your street has the same backup, the problem may be in the public line, and the city's customer service team can say who maintains it.

Before we arrive in DeSoto

Shut the water

Close the main valve at the house. Turn it clockwise until it stops. If water is still flowing, shut the valve at the meter or call the city.

Kill power near water

Switch off breakers to wet rooms only if you can reach the panel safely and stand on a dry surface.

Clear the area

Move people and pets out of the wet space. Lift rugs and valuables if you can do it safely.

Call the 24/7 line

Tell dispatch what you see and where. Photographs help your insurance claim later.
