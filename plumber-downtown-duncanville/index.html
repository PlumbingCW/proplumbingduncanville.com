What Downtown Duncanville is

Downtown Duncanville is a planning district, not a single subdivision. The City of Duncanville Comprehensive Plan, adopted in October 2017, defines the Downtown District as the stretch of North Main Street from Interstate 20 to East Wheatland Road. The same plan describes a separate Main Street District that continues along Main Street from Wheatland Road to US 67.

That makes this page different from the neighborhood pages on this site. Most of the area is commercial and mixed use. A smaller share is residential, and the city housing page names Main Station Lofts, a mixed-use development with lofts above dining and retail, as one of the downtown living options. The City of Duncanville partly owns and funds that project.

What we can say and what we cannot

The Comprehensive Plan says only about half of the 92 acres in the Downtown District are usable land, because a large share is road right of way. It does not publish pipe ages, building ages or plumbing conditions for downtown, so this page does not claim any.

Plumbing for shops, restaurants and offices

Commercial buildings on a main street often include a mix of kitchens, restrooms, mop sinks and floor drains, and the people who run them cannot close for a day while a drain is opened. A slow floor drain, a backed up restroom or a leaking supply line in a shop has a direct cost, so it helps to call early and describe the building.

  • Restaurant and food service drains, including grease related slowdowns and floor drain backups.
  • Restroom repairs such as running toilets, leaking supply lines and failed fixtures.
  • Water heater problems in a small business where hot water is needed all day.
  • Leaks that show up in a ceiling or wall and need to be traced before they spread.

Most of these jobs fall under commercial plumbing and drain cleaning. If a line keeps backing up, a sewer camera inspection shows what is happening inside the pipe before anyone digs.

Lofts and homes near Main Street

Living in a mixed-use building brings its own plumbing questions. In a loft, a leak can affect the unit below or the shop beneath, so it matters to know where your shutoff is and to call as soon as you see water. Building management or the owner decides who may open walls and which shutoffs are shared, so check that before you call.

Homes on the blocks around Main Street are ordinary residential plumbing. A water heater repair, a dripping faucet or a running toilet is the same job here as anywhere in Duncanville. The city overall is described in its Comprehensive Plan as landlocked and largely built out, so most work is repair and replacement rather than new construction.

How to describe a downtown call

  1. 01Say what the building isA shop, a restaurant, an office, a loft or a house. It changes what the plumber brings.
  2. 02Say where the water isA floor drain, a restroom, a kitchen line or a ceiling. Be specific.
  3. 03Find the shutoffKnow where the fixture or building shutoff is before you call.
  4. 04Note accessSay whether the space is open to staff, tenants or the public so the visit can be planned.

Downtown in the city plan

The city Comprehensive Plan lists preserving the historic Main Street and Center City areas as a priority. Plumbing work in a commercial building may need a city permit under the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC) that Duncanville has adopted, so ask Permit and Inspection Services at (972) 780-5000 before a larger job.
