When a tank fails, we remove it and set a new gas or electric unit, often the same day, with a clear price before work starts.

A failed tank cannot be patched, and it often fails at the worst time. If water is spreading from the base, shut the cold supply valve above the heater, turn the gas control to off or flip the breaker, and call. Our 24/7 line schedules replacement the same day when a unit is in stock, and after hours calls are welcome.

Water heater replacement is the removal of a failed unit and the installation of a new one that fits your home. It includes the supply and gas or electric connections, a new relief valve and discharge pipe, venting for gas, and a test. The old tank is hauled away.

When a failed tank is an emergency

A tank that leaks from its body will not stop leaking. Even a small drip can turn into a flood when the tank wall opens. If the heater sits in a closet, attic or a finished garage, a few gallons can ruin flooring and drywall.

Once the water is off, the practical question is how soon you need hot water. Same day replacement is common for standard 40 and 50 gallon tanks. Larger or specialty units may need an order, and we tell you the real timing before you agree.

What a replacement visit includes

  1. 01Shut down and drainWe close the supply, switch off power or gas, and drain the old tank safely.
  2. 02Remove and haulThe old unit is disconnected and carried out, and the pan or floor is checked for damage.
  3. 03Set and connectThe new unit is set level, with new connectors, a new relief valve and a proper discharge pipe.
  4. 04Test and show youWe fill, test for leaks and combustion, set the temperature and show you the shutoffs.

  • Code related partsVents, expansion tank, drain pan and a relief valve discharge pipe add parts where required.
  • LocationA garage replacement is simple. An attic or tight closet takes more labor.

Ask for the price in writing and compare scopes, not just totals.

The expansion tank question

A water heater heats water, and heated water expands. In a closed plumbing system, with a check valve or pressure regulator on the main, that expansion has nowhere to go and pressure climbs. An expansion tank gives the water room. Many new installations include one, and a failing or missing tank can make a relief valve drip.

We explain whether your system is closed and whether the new heater should have an expansion tank, so you do not pay for a part you do not need or skip one you do.
