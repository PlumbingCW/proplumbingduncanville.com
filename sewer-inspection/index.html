Sewer Camera Inspection in Duncanville, TX

A camera on a flexible cable shows what is inside your sewer line before you buy, repair or dig.

Call (972) 903-8224

A sewer camera inspection is a look inside the pipe that runs from your house to the public connection. A small waterproof camera on a flexible cable travels down the line and sends live video to a monitor. The plumber marks problems on the screen and, where useful, along the surface with a locator, so the repair is aimed at the right spot.

Because the pipe is buried, most problems stay hidden until a backup happens. A planned inspection costs far less than an emergency and can turn a vague worry into a clear picture.

When an inspection is worth scheduling

  • Before buying a house built before the 1990s
  • After two or more clogs in the same year
  • Before landscaping, a pool or a driveway job over the line
  • After a drain cleaning that only helped for a short time
  • When you smell sewer gas near the foundation or yard

A home inspection report rarely covers the buried sewer. Duncanville's housing grew from 3,774 residents in 1960 to 27,781 in 1980, so many homes sit on laterals that are older than the buyer. A camera shows the pipe material, the joints and the slope in minutes.

How an inspection works

  1. 01Find the entryWe use the cleanout, or remove a toilet or open a vent, to feed the camera in.
  2. 02Clear a pathIf the line is full of debris, we clean it first, since a dirty lens shows nothing.
  3. 03Record and markWe video the full run, note the depth and distance of each defect and mark the surface when needed.
  4. 04Explain the footageYou see the same video we see, and we tell you what is urgent and what is not.

Inspections are scheduled work, so most appointments are same day or next day when a plumber is free. Emergency backups take priority on the line, and we move straight to drain cleaning when a house is flooding.

What decides the price of an inspection

Cameras are quick on a short, open line and slower on a long or dirty one. You get a clear price before work starts.

  • AccessA cleanout makes it fast. Pulling a toilet adds time and a reset.
  • Line conditionA dirty line must be cleaned first, which is a separate step.
  • Length and depthA long run needs more cable and sometimes a locator to pinpoint depth.
  • Report needsA video copy for a buyer or an insurer takes extra time to prepare.

Ask for the video file and a written summary, whoever does the work. They protect you in a sale or a claim.

Inspections and Duncanville sales

If you are buying in Duncanville, request the inspection during the option period so any finding can be negotiated. Ask the seller whether the sewer lateral has ever been repaired, and ask for any receipts.
